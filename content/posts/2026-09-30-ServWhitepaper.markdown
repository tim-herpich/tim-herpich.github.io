<h2><i class="fas fa-sync-alt"></i> The Challenge</h2>
<p>Insurance policies frequently need to be amended during their lifetime. Changes to a financing structure, repayment schedule, insured amount or cover ratio can result in either a premium surcharge or a premium refund. In some cases, the economic effect of this adjustment is transmitted into the underlying financing transaction: a surcharge is capitalised into the insured credit amount, while a refund can be used to further reduce outstanding insured capital.</p>

<p>In both cases, premium and insured capital become mutually dependent. A capitalised surcharge increases the insured amount and therefore triggers a further surcharge; a refund that reduces insured capital can in turn generate a further refund. This feedback mechanism cannot be handled by conventional pricing frameworks that treat the premium adjustment and insured capital separately.</p>

<p>The paper develops a general mathematical framework for these premium-capital interactions. Building on the Specific Risk Premium introduced in our 2024 whitepaper, it derives a unique analytical solution for the converged premium adjustment and the corresponding cover profile. Rather than modelling each recursive adjustment individually, the framework directly captures its economic end result.</p>

<h2><i class="fas fa-exchange-alt"></i> One Structure, Two Directions</h2>
<p>Premium capitalisation and premium decapitalisation look very different from a business perspective. In the first case, an additional premium surcharge is financed through the export credit. In the second, a premium refund is used to reduce the outstanding insured capital. The central result of the paper is that both are manifestations of the same mathematical structure.</p>

<h2><i class="fas fa-cogs"></i> Why This Matters</h2>
<p>The framework provides a consistent foundation for treating premium-capital interactions in export credit insurance, including both capitalised surcharges and the reduction of insured capital through premium refunds. It also supported the digitalisation of premium calculations in a semi-automated software application.</p>
