    Dealing with the Non-Marketable: Export Credit Insurances

    The Challenge

    Export Credit Agencies (ECAs) insure risks that commercial insurers avoid: long-term, high-risk, and often politically uncertain transactions. While pricing export credit insurance policies is already complex, pricing modifications to these policies is an even greater challenge. There are no regulatory guidelines, no established market benchmarks, and each export case exhibits unique characteristics and therefore idiosyncratic risk profiles.

    A Unified Mathematical Framework

    Colleagues from Schweizerische Exportrisikoversicherung (SERV) and I tackled this challenge in a whitepaper:

    Our whitepaper introduces a mathematical framework that brings structure to this problem. It starts by examining existing models for the initial pricing of export credit insurances and then introduces a central quantity: the Specific Risk Premium. As shown, this quantity allows for a standardized pricing across different export credit insurance modifications by aligning the idiosyncratic risks with the corresponding protection offered by the ECA. The result? A pricing methodology that enables ECAs to apply a unified approach across a diverse range of export business cases — ensuring fairness, efficiency, and better risk management.

    Why This Matters

    In an industry where no two transactions are alike, a structured approach to pricing modifications is essential. Whether extending repayment terms, increasing coverage, or adjusting policy terms post-inception, the ability to price these changes fairly is crucial for ECAs, banks, and exporters.
